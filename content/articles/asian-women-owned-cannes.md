Alia Bhatt showed up for her second consecutive year as L'Oréal Paris's India ambassador and did exactly what she did last year — made Western couture feel secondary. Her opening look: a peach Tamara Ralph gown, structured corset, with a dupatta-inspired stole draped over the arms. Pure Riviera glamour with one deliberate Indian interruption. Then a hand-painted ballgown by artist Basuri Chokshi and textile artist Yash Patil — sage greens, botanical embroidery, a watercolour homage to the French coast that looked like it was designed to be photographed from a hundred metres away. It was.

The look that actually broke the internet wasn't the European couture. It was a saree-inspired ensemble that fashion accounts globally were reposting within hours. India exporting a silhouette that doesn't need explaining — that's the shift. The saree is already one of the most recognisable garments in the world. Alia just reminded the Croisette.

Hoyeon Jung, making her Cannes debut, arrived in custom Louis Vuitton by Nicholas Ghesquière — draped cowl neckline, floral embellishments, built specifically for her. When one of the most commercially valuable faces in luxury fashion makes her festival debut and the house builds her a custom look, that's not a brand deal. That's Louis Vuitton making a statement about where its future is.

BamBam from GOT7 came for the *Karma* premiere in Louis Vuitton monochrome suiting. Thai star Becky Armstrong — *Girl From Nowhere: The Reset* — wore Sophie Couture sculpted taffeta and looked like a fashion week front row and a film premiere simultaneously. Gong Li in Balenciaga. Chloé Zhao as a jury member in a black lace gown, quiet and exact, the way everything she does is quiet and exact.

Asia didn't just show up at Cannes this year. Asia set the tone.
