Jongjin Park's winning piece was sheets of paper layered with coloured porcelain slip, fired in a kiln until the paper burns away and the structure bends under its own weight. Loewe called it *Strata of Illusion*. The Foundation Craft Prize jury called it this year's winner.

The work is extraordinary. But the location is the story.

All finalist works from the 2026 Loewe Foundation Craft Prize are now on display at the National Gallery Singapore — not Madrid, not London, not Seoul. Singapore. For the second year running, one of European luxury's most prestigious craft platforms has chosen this city as its showcase stage.

This is not a coincidence. It is a pattern.

When Jonathan Anderson built the Loewe Foundation Craft Prize into one of fashion's most culturally serious awards, the assumption was that it would orbit the Western art-world axis — the museums, the biennales, the institutions that European luxury has always used to launder commercial power into cultural legitimacy. Anderson made a different choice. He pointed it East. He pointed it specifically at a city that, by all conventional wisdom, shouldn't be a craft capital: no ancient guild tradition, no storied atelier history, no art-school mythology.

What Singapore has instead is infrastructure, stability, and 2,000 family offices whose principals are increasingly interested in cultural legitimacy alongside financial returns. It has a National Gallery that has spent a decade positioning itself as the serious institution in Southeast Asia. And it has a government that understands, with bureaucratic precision, that culture is an asset class.

Loewe isn't doing Singapore a favour. Loewe is reading the room.
